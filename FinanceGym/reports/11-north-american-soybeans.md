# Stats Canada's +10.9% soybean stocks vs. China's 12 MMT commitment: North American export competition and global price stability through Q1 2026

*Point-in-time research note. Evidence cutoff 2025-11-04 UTC. Facts, inference and forward scenarios are labelled separately.*

## Direct answer (inference from admitted evidence)

The two data points are not actually in conflict, and reading them as a conflict overstates the disinflationary punch that Chinese buying could deliver. The +10.9% inventory readings are **backward-looking evidence of a demand failure** (China bought almost no U.S. or marginal Canadian beans during mid-2025), while the 12 MMT commitment is **unverified, forward, calendar-year-2025 U.S.-specific** purchase language announced on 2025-10-30. Through Q1 2026 the most likely outcome is: (1) a **front-loaded, U.S.-skewed export window** in Nov 2025–Jan 2026 that squeezes Canadian and South American bean competition in the same ports; (2) a **narrower, not reversed, inventory overhang**, because commitments of 12 MMT plus 25 MMT/year sit at or below historical norms; and (3) **price stability only through roughly February 2026**, after which Brazil's record crop re-asserts a supply ceiling. Quantitatively, 12 MMT ≈ **441 million bushels** (12 × 36.74 bu/t) — meaningful, but not enough to absorb a North American supply glut of the scale documented below.

## Premise check: which "10.9%"? (fact)

Two separate Statistics Canada releases — both pre-cutoff — reported a **10.9% year-over-year soybean inventory increase**: stocks of **4.2 Mt at December 31, 2024** (on-farm +34.3% to 2.8 Mt, commercial −18.0% to 1.4 Mt), released 2025-02-07 ([statcan.gc.ca](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250207/dq250207c-eng.htm)), and **2.4 Mt at March 31, 2025** (on-farm +11.1%, commercial +10.6% to 988 kt), released 2025-05-08 ([statcan.gc.ca](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250508/dq250508a-eng.htm)). Both describe the **2024-25 crop year** (Canada's soybean crop year runs Sept 1–Aug 31). No Stats Canada inventory reading published on or before the cutoff quantified 2025-26 stocks; the November 2025 Field Crop Survey results were scheduled for **2025-12-04**, after the cutoff ([statcan.gc.ca, 2025-08-28](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250828/dq250828c-eng.htm)). A Sept 2025 Stats Canada inventories report showed total crop-year-end (31 July 2025) inventories **22% lower year-over-year**, driven by canola, wheat, oats and flax drawdowns; the soybean line is not quantified in the admitted excerpt ([Sask Wheat, 2025-09-15](https://saskwheat.ca/wheat-market-outlook/wheat-market-outlook-september-15-2025)). **Analytical consequence:** the +10.9% figure should be treated as a *carried-in* inventory signal, not a current 2025-26 surplus reading.

## Evidence base

**Canadian supply/demand (AAFC outlook, 2025-09-26).** 2024-25 exports were **5.4 Mt (+10%, second-highest on record)** with carry-out stocks a five-year high at **0.56 Mt**; China was the largest buyer of Canadian soybeans at **1.04 Mt**, ahead of Iran (0.77 Mt) and Algeria (0.55 Mt); the track-Chatham average price fell to **$487/t** from $572/t ([producer.com PDF](https://static.producer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025-09-29154258/Canada-Outlook-for-Principal-Field-Crops_202509.pdf)). For 2025-26 AAFC forecast production **7.1 Mt (−6% y/y, fourth-largest on record)**, total supply 8.1 Mt, domestic use 2.2 Mt (−9%), exports 5.4 Mt and carry-out 0.55 Mt.

**The commitment (2025-10-30, Busan).** Per the White House fact sheet as reported: China will buy **at least 12 MMT of U.S. soybeans in the last two months of 2025** and **at least 25 MMT in each of 2026, 2027 and 2028**, plus resumed sorghum/hardwood logs and reduced Chinese tariffs on U.S. farm goods ([AgWeb, 2025-11-04](https://www.agweb.com/news/policy/politics/setting-record-straight-what-china-actually-agreed-buy-and-when-those-ag-pur)). Context: China bought **~23 MMT (841 MMBU)** of U.S. beans in 2024-25 and averaged **29 MMT in 2020-24** ([Iowa Soybean Association timeline, updated 2025-11-03](https://www.iasoybeans.com/newsroom/article/tracking-us-and-china-soybean-trade)). American Farm Bureau (2025-11-03) notes Nov–Dec U.S. exports to China averaged **9.5 MMT** over the last decade, so 12 MMT would be the fourth-highest two-month volume ever, taking calendar-2025 purchases to ~**18 MMT** against a ~**27 MMT** historical annual average — i.e. **33% below trend** ([fb.org](https://www.fb.org/intel/markets/china-phase-two-what-we-know-right-now)).

**Pre-announcement demand hole (facts).** U.S. shipments to China: 985 MMBU in 2024 = 51% of total U.S. soybean exports; only **218 MMBU Jan–Aug 2025** (29% of exports), with June–August effectively zero ([farmdoc daily, 2025-09-22](https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2025/09/us-soybean-harvest-starts-with-no-sign-of-chinese-buying-as-brazil-sets-export-record.html)). USDA projected U.S. ag exports to China of $17bn in 2025 (−30% y/y) and **$9bn in 2026**, the lowest since the 2018 trade war ([fb.org, 2025-10-02](https://www.fb.org/intel/markets/agricultural-trade-china-steps-back-from-u-s-soybeans)). Company-level confirmation of the glut: ADM cut FY2025 adjusted EPS guidance from **$4.00 to $3.25–3.50** (vs $3.84 expected) on 2025-11-04, citing biofuel and trade uncertainty, "awaiting clarity" on the China deal, with press coverage stating "bumper crops in North and South America are filling grain elevators" and China's limited purchases "created a supply glut" ([Finnhub/ADM news](https://finnhub.io/api/news?id=80a2170936c76c79a4c9f342e453f9414d2278d75e6f2256c00538c2a4f22a6a), [2](https://finnhub.io/api/news?id=b352e9bd3164c8f828e3b64899e851ba485c9c973685bbbef16a2e2bc10d7fce), [3](https://finnhub.io/api/news?id=36d51c8c4d9d449b9f1381b9f3c90a2d39f15e9eee3b13cfa0e8c8bd424ac6b4)).

**Price and basis evidence.** CBOT November soybeans traded around **$10.30¾/bu** (peak $10.38, highest since Sept 19) on 2025-10-22 ([ukragroconsult](https://ukragroconsult.com/en/news/us-soybean-prices-rise-amid-expectations-of-renewed-chinese-purchases)); ISA reports the bushel price rose **more than 60¢ on the Oct 30 news**, Northern Plains and port **basis ticked up**, and **U.S. port prices moved close to Brazilian levels** — while, as of Nov 1, **China had signed no contract** ([iasoybeans.com](https://www.iasoybeans.com/newsroom/article/tracking-us-and-china-soybean-trade)). Scott Irwin (2025-10-20) estimated that adjusting for non-Chinese destinations, total 2025-26 exports were closer to **1.43–1.47 bn bu vs USDA's 1.685 bn bu**, implying roughly **+250 MMBU** to ending stocks and ~**500 MMBU (12.5% stocks-to-use)** ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/scotthirwin_soybeans-china-trade-activity-7386034964032790528-f2Np)). FX: USDCAD moved from ~1.3738 (2025-09-01) to ~1.4055 (2025-11-04), a **+2.3% Canadian-dollar depreciation** (OpenBB/yfinance daily observation — retrospective vintage, not a frozen publication), which improves CAD-denominated farmer economics and modestly sharpens Canadian USD offer prices.

## Mechanisms and scenarios for Q1 2026 (inference)

1. **Demand redirection, not demand creation.** Chinese buying concentrates in the Pacific Northwest and Gulf in Nov–Jan. Because U.S. port prices are now near Brazilian parity, Canadian beans (net-backs out of Chatham/Thunder Bay, and Pacific routing) lose marginal price competitiveness precisely in China's historical Nov–Mar purchase window. **Inference:** Canadian 2025-26 exports of 5.4 Mt become harder in Q1, with carry-out stocks drifting toward/above the 0.55 Mt forecast, echoing the +10.9% build pattern of early 2025.
2. **Offsetting Canadian lever.** A weaker CAD plus non-China demand (Iran, Algeria, Bangladesh/Vietnam-type crush buyers) partially cushions volume; AAFC's Chatham price forecast was already "up slightly" for 2025-26.
3. **Window closing.** Brazil's Conab projected a record **177.6 Mt** for 2025-26 (USDA 175 Mt; private 176.5–181 Mt) with record **~112 Mt exports**, roughly matching China's total forecast imports ([Michigan Farm News, 2025-10-16](https://www.michiganfarmnews.com/brazil-projects-record-soybean-crop-and-growing-exports-citing-demand-from-china); [Terrain, 2025-10-06](https://www.terrainag.com/insights/can-the-u-s-crush-the-competition)). Chinese buyers also booked Brazilian cargoes for December and March–July 2026, and Argentine soy after Buenos Aires suspended its 26% export tax following the U.S. support package — which ISA said let China "bypass U.S. soybean farmers through February 2026." **Inference:** from Feb–Mar 2026 the marginal buyer switches back to South America, capping any Q1 rally.
4. **Scenarios (probabilities are judgemental, not modelled).** *Base (~55%):* partial delivery, 17–20 MMT CY2025 Chinese purchases, CBOT ranges $9.80–$11.00/bu, basis firm in the U.S. and soft in Canada; moderate price stability. *Bull-for-North America (~25%):* full 12 MMT delivered plus early 25 MMT 2026 signing; U.S. exports near 1.6 bn bu, stocks nearer 400 MMBU, prices bid above $11 but with freight/river-logistics bottlenecks and a sharper post-Brazil-correction decline in Q2. *Bear (~20%):* headlines without contracts (as at Nov 1); futures unwind toward Sept lows, and the inventory overhang persists — the pattern ADM's guidance cut already priced.

## Counterarguments and residual risks

Beijing had not publicly confirmed the commitment at cutoff, and Phase One precedent shows gradual diversion to other suppliers. Record U.S. domestic crush competes for the same bushels (biofuel-policy clarity not expected before spring 2026), muting exportable surplus. The U.S. government shutdown delayed official statistics (noted 2025-11-04), degrading shipment verification. China's import demand may be nearer 100 MMT than USDA's 112 MMT, capping every exporter.

## Evidence gaps

Stats Canada's 2025-26 production/stocks detail (2025-12-04 release) and November 2025 U.S. export-sales/inspection data fall outside the cutoff; exact Nov 3–4 January-2026 CBOT settlements and Chinese contract confirmations were unavailable in admitted sources; no frozen-vintage WASDE/FRED soybean price series was accessible, so prices come from dated news documents.
