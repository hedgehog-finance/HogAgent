# Edison International (EIX): Divergent Signals, Wildfire Tail Risk and the 2025 Risk-Adjusted Outlook

**Point-in-time evidence cutoff: 2025-05-27.** Facts below come only from documents published on or before that date. Direct SEC/issuer data was pulled via the Finnhub Form 4 feed and Fed H(15) Treasury curves via OpenBB; all market-series observations are retrospective records, not frozen publication vintages.

## 1. Direct answer

The "contradiction" in the premise is largely an artifact of how insider activity is reported. Form 4 data through 2025-04-28 shows only **5,827 shares of genuine open-market insider selling (~$0.40 million) between 2024-11-04 and 2025-03-06** — about **0.0015%** of the 385 million weighted-average shares outstanding (https://newsroom.edison.com/releases/edison-international-reports-first-quarter-2025-results). By contrast, **250,473 shares (~$17.4 million gross) of apparent "selling" were code-F tax-withholding dispositions tied to scheduled option exercises** in two non-discretionary batches: 155,499 shares withheld on 2025-01-02 at $80.01 and 94,973 on 2025-02-26 at $52.61 — roughly **43x** the volume of discretionary sales, with option strikes as low as $63.72. The signal gap is therefore narrow: modest, mechanical insider distribution versus fundamentals that genuinely improved after the February 2025 TKM settlement.

Risk-adjusted, EIX at the cutoff was a **cheap-cash-flow, high-tail-risk** equity: consensus-quality earnings ($5.94–$6.34 core EPS guided) at roughly 8.9x guidance midpoint, a maintained $0.8275 quarterly dividend (≈6.1% yield derived from a ~$54.5 implied price), and a 5–7% 2025–2028 core-EPS CAGR to $6.74–$7.14 — set against an Eaton Fire liability that management moved to "probable" material loss on 2025-04-29, with an unresolved cause investigation (12–18 months), ~$21 billion of shared state-fund backstop capacity and a $1 billion self-insured retention. Wildfire tail risk, not 2025 operating earnings, dominates the distribution of outcomes; the expected value is supported, the variance is not.

## 2. Evidence behind each signal

**Fundamental value tests (passed, with a real driver).** On 2025-02-27 Edison revised 2025 core EPS guidance **upward to $5.94–$6.34 from $5.50–$5.90** after CPUC approval of the TKM settlement, which authorized **$1.6 billion of cost recovery for pre-AB 1054 wildfire claims** (https://newsroom.edison.com/releases/edison-international-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-results). Q1 2025 (2025-04-29): net income **$1,436M / GAAP $3.73**, core **$528M / $1.37** versus $1.13 a year earlier (**+21.2%**), on ~$3.8B revenue; guidance affirmed; the prior-year Q1 was a $11M loss. Wildfire exposure is heavily capitalized on the balance sheet: regulatory assets rose to **$10,548M** (from $8,886M), the Wildfire Insurance Fund contribution asset was **$1,844M**, while the recorded "wildfire-related claims" liability was only **$55M** and cash was **$1,318M** after short-term debt fell from $998M to $5M (https://www.edison.com/_gallery/get_file?file_id=68112bb43d63329aeb68f9d8&file_ext=.pdf). Zacks (2025-03-26) flagged a **Value Style Score of A** with a **forward P/E of 9.77**, four analysts raising FY25 estimates and consensus up $0.11 to $5.86 (https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2435470/heres-why-edison-international-eix-is-a-strong-value-stock).

**Analyst upgrades (real, but paired with lower targets).** Barclays moved EIX to **Overweight from Equalweight on 2025-01-02 while cutting the target to $84 from $91**, citing wildfire/regulatory risk mitigation (https://www.investing.com/news/analyst-ratings/barclays-lifts-edison-international-stock-rating-cuts-price-target-93CH-3793671). Ladenburg Thalmann upgraded to **Neutral from Sell** on 2025-01-16 with a **$56.50** target reflecting "worst-case" fire outcomes; Zacks simultaneously **downgraded** to neutral (https://www.latimes.com/business/story/2025-01-16/edison-stock-turns-volatile-as-growing-blame-for-wildfires-lands-on-the-power-company). Upgrades here mean "less bad than feared," not undiminished risk.

**Insider activity (primary filings).** Beyond the withholding flows above, discretionary sales were: 1,250 shares by Taylor at $83.28 (2024-11-15), 1,287 by Bowman at $87.88 (2024-11-22), and 3,288 by director Beliveau-Dunn at $54.34 on 2025-03-06, taking her reported holding to **zero**. January's exercises included CEO Pizarro's 150,403 option shares (111,608 + 38,795). Filing dates cluster around scheduled vesting windows, and April 2025 filings are exclusively code-A derivative awards — no post-disclosure discretionary selling is visible in the admitted data through 2025-04-28.

**Institutional accumulation (not verified).** Finnhub company-news queries returned **no admitted items**, and no dated pre-cutoff 13F aggregate source was retrievable; the premise remains **unevidenced**. Index ownership and a ~6% derived yield are consistent with, but do not prove, net accumulation.

**Wildfire liabilities (the binding constraint).** The Eaton Fire ignited 2025-01-07, burned ~14,021 acres, destroyed ~9,400 structures, damaged 1,000+ and killed 17–18 people (https://recovery.lacounty.gov/2025-03-05/sue-edison-eaton-fire; https://abcnews.com/US/la-county-pasadena-sierra-madre-sue-socal-edison-eaton-fire/story?id=119485447). LA County, Pasadena and Sierra Madre sued on 2025-03-05, the County estimating "at least hundreds of millions" for itself; a plaintiffs' coalition reports 130+ suits (https://www.robertkinglawfirm.com/california-wildfire-attorneys/southern-california-edison-fire-lawsuit). In the 2024 Annual Report (2025-02-27) SCE still "ha[d] not determined whether its equipment was associated with the ignition" and could not estimate a loss range (https://download.edison.com/406/files/202503/2024-eix-sce-annual-report.pdf). By 2025-04-29, Pizarro called material loss **probable**: circumstantial facts plus the absence of any competing ignition hypothesis (https://www.tdworld.com/utility-business/article/55287467/ceo-edison-international-will-probably-take-material-losses-due-to-eaton-fire; https://www.utilitydive.com/news/southern-california-edison-sce-eaton-fire/746965). Mitigants: **AB 1054** safety certificate and liability cap, $1.0B customer-funded self-insurance (with up to $12.5M shareholder contribution), then Wildfire Insurance Fund reimbursement subject to the administrator and ~$21B shared capacity; rebuild capex guidance of **$860–$925M**; authorized CPUC ROE 10.33% and FERC ROE 10.30% (https://www.edison.com/_gallery/get_file?file_id=68112c603d63329acd68f9d8&file_ext=.pdf). The market repriced hard: shares fell ~9% on the earnings commentary, were down >30% over six months, and market cap was ~$21 billion on 2025-05-01.

## 3. Mechanisms reconciling the divergence

(1) **Regulatory cash recovery converts litigation reserves into financed receivables** — TKM recovery lowered interest expense (~14¢ run-rate), lifting guidance without changing the probability of SCE responsibility. (2) **Compensation mechanics, not information, generate most insider "sales"**; executives exercised deeply in-the-money options at $80 in January, months before the April probable-loss language, so selling is not evidence of foreknowledge. (3) **The equity is a levered claim on a shared, finite public backstop**: with $21B fund capacity shared among three utilities versus a ~$21B equity market cap, a single catastrophic season can be survivable, but multi-event or fund-inadequate scenarios transmit directly to shareholders — hence management's lobbying on fund expansion. (4) **Valuation is a rate and litigation-timeline discount**, not a solvency judgment: 10-year yields eased from 4.58% (2025-01-31) to 4.51% (2025-05-23) while 30-year yields rose from 4.83% to 5.04%, so financing costs at the long end still pressure allowed-returns math.

## 4. Counterarguments

Bulls can argue the accrual gap is conservative — wildfire claims booked at low-end ranges historically resolve favorably after settlements, and the 10-Q shows almost no Eaton accrual yet, so "probable" may be already-priced at 8.9x guidance. Bears reply that guidance deliberately excludes Eaton losses ("Basic EPS guidance only incorporates non-core items to March 31, 2025"), that Pizarro declined to cap damages, that the $21B fund may be exhausted before trial dates, and that a negligence finding or a safety-certificate challenge would void AB 1054 protection and force debt issuance at the holding company — the very risk that produced the 23% January drawdown.

## 5. Scenarios (2025 calendar year, forward-looking)

- **Base (most likely):** Fund access confirmed, claims settle within fund capacity; core EPS in $5.94–$6.34, dividend maintained; no Eaton charge to core; stock driven by cost-of-capital and rate-base news. Sensitivity: each **$1B** of after-tax (at an assumed 25% rate) uninsured net cost ≈ **$1.95/share**, ≈ **32% of the guidance midpoint**.
- **Downside:** Fund inadequacy, negligence finding, or a damages number above third-party estimates; even $5B net after-tax ≈ **$9.73/share**, and forced holding-company debt plus dividend pressure would re-rate the ~6% yield.
- **Upside:** Legislation expanding the fund plus continued TKM-style recoveries; 2028 CAGR toward $7.14 with the wildfire overhang narrowed.

## 6. Risk-adjusted conclusion

Verified facts support a business with improving regulatory economics and a valuation discount that reflects tail risk rather than operational decay. Once insider "selling" is decomposed, it carries almost no information content; the unresolved premises are whether institutions were truly accumulating and whether the state backstop holds. On the evidence at the cutoff, 2025 earnings risk is manageable while **liability-financing risk remains unquantified** — the risk-adjusted outlook is neutral-to-constructive on expected earnings, and materially impaired on worst-case variance.

## 7. Limitations

Finnhub company news returned empty results (coverage gap, not absence of news); Form 4 coverage ends 2025-04-28 filing date; no pre-cutoff 13F, ownership-flow, rating-distribution or price-history source was obtainable within budget; the $54.49 price, 6.07% yield, 8.87x multiple, and per-share loss sensitivities (25% tax assumption) are **derived**, not quoted; Treasury curves are retrospective H(15) observations; plaintiffs' lawsuit counts come from law-firm sources.
