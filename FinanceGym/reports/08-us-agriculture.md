# USDA's August 2025 Soybean Revisions and the Labor Day Boxed Beef Spike: Read‑Through to Q3 Operating Margins for U.S. Livestock and Feed Processors

**Evidence cutoff: 2025-09-02 (inclusive). Facts below are drawn only from documents published on or before that date.**

## Direct answer

The two shocks are **not symmetric in magnitude, and they do not hit the same cost line**. The USDA's 12 August 2025 revisions cut soybean acreage and production (a mildly *inflationary* signal for meal-based feed costs), while the boxed beef cutout's surge above $400/cwt into Labor Day was a far larger, but *mixed*, event: it raised revenue per pound while fed cattle costs sat at records, so it improved **gross spreads without by itself restoring positive packer margins**. On pre-cutoff evidence, the most defensible projection for **calendar Q3 2025** is: (i) **beef packers narrow but do not close losses** (cash margin estimates were still **‑$181.50/head in late July**; the August cutout gain is worth roughly **$234–469/head of carcass value** if it sticks); (ii) **branded/value-added processors stay under pressure** — Hormel cut FY25 adjusted EPS guidance on 28 Aug, blaming "the steep rise in commodity input costs"; (iii) **feed-dependent poultry and hog integrators get modest relief**, because the soybean revision was largely swamped by a record corn crop and meal prices still **below $300/ton**. Protein-processing margins in Q3 should read as *better than Q2 but still well below the branded-food benchmark*: roughly **3–4% adjusted operating margin for multi-protein integrators versus ~8% for branded processors** (Tyson 3.6% adjusted vs Hormel 8.4% in the latest reported quarters).

## Fact base

**1. USDA August revisions (published 12 Aug 2025).** NASS cut soybean **planted acreage from 83.4 to 80.9 million acres** while raising corn from 95.2 to 97.3 million ([farmdoc daily, 18 Aug](https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2025/08/yes-there-are-a-lot-of-corn-acres-evidence-from-fsa-acreage-data.html)). WASDE-663 projected soybean **harvested area of 80.1 million acres (‑2.4 million m/m)** on a **record 53.6 bu/ac (+1.1)**, giving 2025/26 production of **~4.3 billion bushels, ‑43 million vs July and ‑290 million vs last year's 4.36 billion**; beginning stocks ‑20 million bu, exports ‑40 million bu to 1.7 billion, crush unchanged at 2.54 billion, **ending stocks 290 million bu (‑20 m/m; 6.3% stocks/use)** at an unchanged $10.10/bu season-average price ([American Farm Bureau, 12 Aug](https://www.fb.org/intel/markets/wasde-robust-crops-high-input-costs-strained-margins)). The market read was bullish-soybeans/bearish-corn: **November soybeans closed +21¢ at $10.32** while **December corn fell 13¢ to $3.94**, the first sub-$4 close for a 2025 corn contract, with corn output raised to a **record 16.7 billion bu** and ending stocks of 2.1 billion bu, the highest since 2018/19 (same source). Soybean production was "at the low end of pre-report estimates," with world ending stocks trimmed 1.17 MMT to 124.9 MMT ([Grain Farmers of Ontario, 18 Aug](https://gfo.ca/market-trends/market-trends-report-august-september-2025)).

**2. Boxed beef, Labor Day run.** The USDA Choice 600–900 lb cutout was **$400.57/cwt for the week ending 15 Aug (+$21.73 w/w, +$27.02 m/m, +26.2% y/y vs $317.45; 5-year average $289.06)**; Select was $370.76, the Choice/Select spread widened $6.06 to $29.81, and the CME Feeder Cattle Index reached **345.75 (52-week high 346.01 on 13 Aug; 322.28 a month earlier; 246.71 a year earlier)** ([Cattle Range Weekly Market Summary, 15 Aug](https://cattlerange.com/static/assets/3gdp4btx/wms_08-15-25m.pdf)). LMIC confirms the holiday mechanism: the cutout "turned higher the first week of August and reached above $400 per cwt by the end of the month, **largely driven by demand leading up to the Labor Day Holiday**. The last time the boxed beef cutout value topped $400 per cwt was late May 2020" ([LMIC Margins and Markets, Aug 2025](https://lmic.info/wp-content/lmic-files/lmic-data/tac/publications/Margins%20and%20Markets/2025/Margins%20and%20Markets%2008-27-25.pdf)). Trade commentary put August's monthly gain at **+$54.09, higher in 17 of 21 sessions — "the best August in history"** ([National Beef Wire, 1 Sep](https://www.facebook.com/NationalBeefWireDVAuction/posts/1520663878905007)).

**3. Where margins stood entering Q3.** Sterling Marketing had **packer margins at ‑$181.50/head** in the post-July-4 week (from ‑$208.72 a month earlier), a comprehensive beef cutout averaging $376.86, slaughter of 563,000 head/wk, **867 lb carcasses**, 79.0% cutout utilization, and a **full-year projection of ‑$205/head** ([Meatingplace, 28 Jul](https://meatingplace.com/beef-packers-face-an-unprofitable-2025)). Structural supply, per LMIC: **July 1 cattle inventory 94.2 million head, the lowest in the report's history**; Aug 1 feedlot inventory **10.9 million (‑1.6% y/y)** with July marketings **‑5.7%** and placements **‑6.1%**; the Mexico cattle border closed for New World Screwworm; **H1 beef imports +33% to 2.9 billion lb**, exports ‑8%; **all-fresh retail beef $8.90/lb in July, a record, +9% y/y**.

**4. Company-reported margins (both pre-cutoff).** Tyson (FY25 Q3 ended 31 May, reported **4 Aug**): sales $13.884 bn (+4.0%), **GAAP operating margin 1.9%, adjusted 3.6%**, adjusted EPS $0.91, a **$343 million Beef goodwill impairment**, FY25 adjusted operating income **$2.1–2.3 bn** with **Beef adjusted loss $(475)–$(375) million**, Pork $175–200 million, Chicken $1.3–1.4 bn, Prepared $925 million–$1.0 bn ([Tyson, 4 Aug](https://www.tysonfoods.com/news/news-releases/2025/8/tyson-foods-reports-third-quarter-2025-results)). Hormel (Q3 FY25 ended 27 Jul, reported **28 Aug**): sales $3.03 bn (+6% organic), **operating margin 7.9%, adjusted 8.4%**, segment profit ‑4% Retail / ‑1% Foodservice / ‑13% International, FY25 adjusted EPS guidance **cut to $1.43–$1.45 from $1.58–$1.68**, and Q4 (Aug–Oct) adjusted EPS **$0.38–$0.40** with "near-term pressures … persisting through the fourth quarter" ([Hormel, 28 Aug](https://www.hormelfoods.com/newsroom/press-releases/hormel-foods-reports-third-quarter-fiscal-2025-results)).

## Mechanisms (inference)

**(a) Beef: a value spike, not a margin gift.** The cutout is a wholesale *value* index, not a spread. At 867 lb carcass weight, the month-over-month cutout rise of $27.02/cwt equates to **≈$234/head of added carcass value**, and the whole August move (+$54.09) to **≈$469/head** — the same order of magnitude as the ‑$205/head full-year loss projection. Against that, feeder and fed cattle prices were rising simultaneously (CME Feeder Index +7.3% m/m; the $113/cwt y/y cutout gain coincided with a $99 y/y feeder-index gain), so **the realized Q3 improvement is the difference of two rallies**. LMIC's read is that strength "is likely a signal that fed cattle prices will be supported" — i.e. packers pay much of it back at the scale.

**(b) Feed: second-order.** Using published finishing-ratio sensitivities (**each $0.10/bu corn ≈ $0.43/cwt feed cost; each $10/ton meal ≈ $0.37/cwt**; [farmdoc daily, 6 Dec 2024](https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2024/12/prospects-for-swine-feed-costs-in-2025.html)), a $10/ton meal move is **~0.4% of a ~$170/cwt hog cost basis**. A 1% production cut (‑43 million of 4.3 billion bushels) and 6.3% stocks/use are therefore small next to the **record corn crop**. LMIC's own feed picture is supportive: corn "flirt[ing] with $4/bushel," **meal and hay below $300 and $200/ton**, and **July farrow-to-finish returns of $51.90/head (best since June 2021) on total feed cost of $86.25/head (‑7% y/y, meal ‑25%)**. The relevant feed-processor risk is **crush**, not feed cost: meal was already weak enough that "low meal prices pose risks to soybean crush margins," with SOYBEX FOB Santos at $449.58/mt (+12% y/y) on 22 Aug ([S&P Global, 26 Aug](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/agriculture/082625-low-meal-prices-pose-risks-to-soybean-crush-margins-in-south-america)); the acreage cut firms beans and meal, but USDA's ‑40 million bu export revision and unchanged 2.54 billion bu crush keep net crush economics thin for integrators that own the crush.

## Counterarguments and scenarios

*Counter to the bullish read:* the spike is **calendar-driven and reversible**, with wholesale values historically reaching a pre-Labor Day zenith followed by restocking support only; **record $8.90/lb retail beef** invites substitution (LMIC notes high beef and pork prices are pushing consumers to chicken at $2.43–2.48/lb); **imports +33%** and heavier carcasses blunt scarcity; and an independent crop-condition model using 11 Aug ratings estimated 4.2–4.4 billion bu, i.e. the production cut could unwind ([AgManager, 16 Aug](https://agmanager.info/sites/default/files/pdf/SoybeanYield_Conditions_08-18-25.pdf)). Tyson's **$343 million Beef goodwill impairment** shows the segment's problem is asset-level, not one holiday week.

- **Base:** cutout mean-reverts toward $370–400 after the holiday; Q3 packer cash margins improve from ‑$181.50/head toward ‑$50 to +$50/head; Tyson holds FY25 Beef loss guidance within $(475)–$(375) million — ≈**2.1% of FY24 Beef sales**, i.e. a segment operating margin near **‑2%**; multi-protein adjusted operating margin ~**3.5–4%**, branded processors ~**8%** with commodity drag persisting into Q4.
- **Upside:** demand and trim/export strength holds the cutout above $410 into September while fed cattle plateau → positive packer spreads, Beef losses narrowing toward $(375) million, and chicken/pork helped by lower feed.
- **Downside:** post-holiday retracement plus record fed cattle ("brutal second half," Sterling's ‑$205/head), consumer resistance at record retail, tariff/trade shocks (exports already ‑8%), and a rebound to 4.3–4.4 billion bu of soybeans that lifts meal supply, hurting crush-dependent feed operators even as integrators' feed costs fall.

## Uncertainties and evidence gaps

"Q3" is defined inconsistently: **Tyson's fiscal Q4 (Jun 29–Aug 30) and Hormel's fiscal Q4 (Aug 3–Nov 2) — not their reported Q3s — overlap calendar Q3**, so the calendar-quarter statements above are **inference from monthly market data and issuer guidance, not reported results**. Commodity price series retrieved after the cutoff are retrospective vintages, so all beef and feed figures here come from dated documents; the 12 Aug WASDE/NASS values are as-published and unrevised pre-cutoff. **Coverage gaps:** the Finnhub skill returned **no company news for TSN/TYSON (15 Jul–2 Sep) and no insider transactions (1 Jul–2 Sep; 3 records excluded as post-cutoff)**, and OpenBB was not queried because livestock and boxed-beef prices are outside its documented endpoints. There is therefore **no admitted pre-cutoff evidence for National Beef/JBS, Cargill, ADM, Bunge or Darling**, and no daily cutout series for the exact 25 Aug–2 Sep holiday week (weekly and monthly averages used instead). No claim is made about actual reported Q3 results, which did not exist by 2025-09-02.
