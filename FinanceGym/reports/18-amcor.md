# Amcor–Berry: What the $2.2bn Notes Really Change for Leverage and Competitive Position (evidence as of 16 Mar 2025)

## Direct answer

On the evidence available by 16 March 2025, the $2.2 billion senior unsecured notes priced by Amcor on 13 March 2025 do **not** raise the combined group's total debt burden — they *term out and re-domesticate* Berry Global's change-of-control indebtedness into Amcor's investment-grade capital structure ahead of a closing then expected in "mid calendar year 2025" (https://www.amcor.com/media/news/amcor-and-berry-global-receive-us-antitrust-clearance-for-combination, 11 Mar 2025; https://www.amcor.com/media/news/amcor-prices-private-offering-of-2-pt-2-billion-of-senior-unsecured-notes, 13 Mar 2025). Pro-forma leverage therefore stays where management had already told the market it would be — around 3.3x net debt/adjusted EBITDA at close, with a stated path below 3.0x within the first full year (https://www.amcor.com/media/news/amcor-and-berry-to-combine-in-an-all-stock-transaction, 19 Nov 2024) — while the *quality* of the liability improves: fixed-rate, unsecured, 3–10 year maturities priced at roughly 87–123bp over the March 13, 2025 U.S. Treasury curve. Competitive positioning strengthens through scale and portfolio mix rather than through balance-sheet capacity, and the principal risks are integration-synergy delivery, a still-outstanding European Commission clearance, and the reduced M&A/dividend headroom that ~3.3x leverage implies.

## What the $2.2bn changes in the capital structure

The S-4 pro formas disclose Berry's debt of **$8.3bn at 28 Sept 2024**, of which **~$7.0bn** was assumed to carry into the merger; $0.8bn matured in January 2025 and ~$0.5bn was to be repaid from Berry cash (including proceeds of the healthcare solutions segment disposal). Amcor assumed a **$1.8bn draw on its $3.0bn 364-day unsecured bridge** (committed 19 Nov 2024 by Goldman Sachs Bank USA and UBS) to repay "Specified Berry Indebtedness" triggered by change of control, and a **combined cash target of $750m** (S-4, 13 Jan 2025, URL above).

Against that blueprint, the notes are a *substitute*, not an addition: $2.2bn of permanent capital covers ~73% of the $3.0bn bridge commitment and ~122% of the $1.8bn bridge draw contemplated in the pro formas. Mechanically this (i) removes 364-day refinancing/reset risk from the closing balance sheet, (ii) caps a meaningful slice of combined debt at fixed coupons, and (iii) confirms market access at investment-grade spreads.

Cost arithmetic (from the pricing terms): weighted-average coupon = (725×4.80 + 725×5.10 + 750×5.50)/2200 = **5.1375%**, i.e. ≈ **$113m of annual cash interest** on the new notes. Benchmarking to the Federal Reserve H15 curve for 13 Mar 2025 (3y 3.93%, 5y 4.02%, 10y 4.27%; retrieved via OpenBB as a retrospective observation, not a frozen vintage): implied new-issue spreads of **+87bp (2028), +108bp (2030), +123bp (2035)**, weighted ≈ **+106bp** — tight, mid-investment-grade territory, and the clearest market verdict available before cutoff on the combined credit.

## Competitive positioning into mid-2025

- **Scale and mix**: ~$24bn combined sales, >$3bn combined annual cash flow, ~$4.3bn adjusted EBITDA, customers in >140 countries via ~400 plants; ~60% Flexibles, ~40% scaled containers & closures, plus a global healthcare portfolio (Yahoo/Zacks 14 Mar 2025 and the 19 Nov 2024 release, URLs above).
- **Category leverage**: management frames the combination as shifting the portfolio toward "high-growth, high-margin" healthcare, protein, pet food, liquids, beauty & personal care and food service — the axis on which the combined entity competes against specialist peers rather than on commodity volume.
- **Consolidation context**: Amcor–Berry sits inside a 2024–25 wave — Smurfit Kappa/WestRock, International Paper/DS Smith, Sonoco/Eviosys, Novolex/Pactiv Evergreen — in which scale, not price, is the competitive lever (https://www.packagingstrategies.com/articles/105286-packaging-m-and-a-signals-industry-growth-and-strategic-consolidation-through-2025, 18 Dec 2024). That source also pegs Berry at ~1.5x sales / ~9x EBITDA versus Amcor's ~11x five-year average EV/EBITDA, i.e. the combined entity is built by buying scale below its own multiple.
- **Credit as a commercial asset**: an IG-rated, unsecured, laddered structure at ~5.1% average coupon supports customer and supplier confidence in a capital-intensive sector and preserves some capacity for bolt-on portfolio reshaping, while the "commitment to investment grade balance sheet and continued annual dividend growth from" an annualised $0.51 per share (19 Nov 2024 release) is the equity-market contract constraining it.

## Counterarguments and risks

1. **Leverage relief is optical**: the notes refinance existing Berry debt; they do not deleverage. Until synergies land, the group carries ~3.3x with ~$113m of new coupon, plus residual Berry obligations needing repayment or refinancing.
2. **Execution/conditionality**: the merger had not closed at cutoff. The S-4 listed non-U.S. antitrust approvals among remaining conditions, and on 11 March the HSR expiry was described as satisfying "another closing condition"; special mandatory redemption of the 2030s/2035s therefore remained live if closing slipped past the Outside Date. (Amcor's newsroom later surfaced an EC unconditional approval and an April 30, 2025 closing target — outside this cutoff and excluded from the analysis.)
3. **Demand backdrop**: Amcor's organic volumes were flat-to-down (Q2 net sales $3,241m vs $3,251m; H1 $6,594m vs $6,694m), so deleveraging depends on EBITDA conversion and cash flow, not growth. Customer-retention and key-person risks during integration are explicitly flagged in Amcor's own forward-looking statements.
4. **Regulatory after-effects**: an unchallenged HSR expiry is not a judgment on market power; conditions or remedies elsewhere, or later scrutiny in containers/closures, could still alter the competitive thesis.

## Scenarios leading into mid-2025

- **Base (best supported)**: closing mid-2025; pro-forma net leverage ~3.2–3.4x, easing toward 3.0x as ~$650m of synergies phase in and $900m–$1,000m FY25 adjusted FCF is applied; IG ratings affirmed; top-tier global position in flexible plus rigid consumer packaging consolidated.
- **Upside**: faster synergy capture plus the disclosed $280m one-time working-capital benefit drives leverage below 3.0x inside the first year and restores bolt-on M&A capacity.
- **Downside**: outstanding regulatory or other conditions delay closing; the 2030s/2035s are specially redeemed, Amcor bears bridge/commitment economics longer, and ~3.3x leverage is absorbed by soft volumes — leaving the group more levered and less flexible than standalone Amcor would have been.
