A second premise refinement: in mid-2025 Feldman **downgraded Target** but **did not downgrade Best Buy**. What he did to both was **cut estimates and targets**; only Target lost its rating.

## Direct answer

Feldman's May–June 2025 actions encode a **trade-down bifurcation**: money flowing *into* value, consumables and membership-fee formats (Walmart, Costco) and *out of* discretionary, big-ticket and non-price-driven formats (Target, Best Buy, Deckers), with tariffs converting that mix shift into an earnings-risk event for the second group. The divergence is visible in **direction of revisions**, not in headline valuations: his cut names actually screen *cheaper* on upside-to-target than his favourites.

## Mechanisms

1. **Tariff pass-through hits import-heavy discretionary baskets first.** Both cut firms embedded tariffs in guidance; Walmart's CFO called tariffs "still too high" but kept FY guidance, leaning on scale, 5,000+ price rollbacks and private-brand penetration (+60bps).
2. **Trade-down is zero-sum on traffic.** Value, consumables and membership formats gained share; Target's non-consumables did not. Best Buy's services comped +0.9% while appliances (-8.1%) and electronics (-5.2%) fell — big-ticket is cut first.
3. **Execution visibility.** Feldman cited inconsistent execution; Target's CEO described running "week by week, almost day by day," amid an executive shake-up and boycott-driven traffic loss.
4. **Membership income de-risks forecasting** for Costco/Walmart in a way absent at Target/Best Buy.

## Counterarguments

- **Upside-to-target contradicts a "bearish discretionary" label.** Implied 12-month upside at report-date prices: Best Buy **+27.2%** ($90/$70.73), Target **+18.3%** ($110/$93.01), Walmart **+18.0%** ($115/$97.46), Costco **+9.6%** ($1,100/$1,003.91). His least-favoured name screened cheapest — the divergence is in confidence in the estimate path, not price attractiveness.
- **Not a clean staples-vs-discretionary split.** He kept Outperform on discretionary housing names (Home Depot $455, Lowe's $305) yet held Dollar General/Dollar Tree at Market Perform despite raising targets. Tariff exposure, ticket size and execution explain his actions better than sector labels.
- **Target's gap is partly self-inflicted** (DEI pullback, boycotts), so part of the Walmart–Target spread is idiosyncratic, not consumer bifurcation.
- **Single-analyst, aggregator-recorded evidence**; ratings are ordinal and non-standardised.
