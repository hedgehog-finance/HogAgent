# Zebra Technologies (NASDAQ: ZBRA): Why a Q1 EPS Beat Could Coexist with a Bearish Valuation Reset

**Information cutoff: 2025-06-10 (UTC, inclusive).** Analysis uses only documents retrievable on or before that date.

## Direct answer

The parts of the question that are verifiable before the cutoff point to a coherent bearish mechanism, but **two premises of the question could not be verified with pre-cutoff evidence**: (i) specific Truist and Barclays June 2025 price-target reductions, and (ii) the reported Norges Bank / GAMMA Investing inflows (see "Evidence gaps").

What *is* documented: Zebra beat on EPS in Q1 2025 (non-GAAP diluted EPS **$4.02** vs. a Zacks consensus of **$3.60**; net sales $1,308M, +11.3% YoY, +11.9% organic) — yet the same filings expose (a) a **low-quality-tangible, goodwill-funded capital structure** in which $2,183M of debt sits on $3,626M of book equity that is more than fully accounted for by intangibles, and (b) **deceleration concentrated in exactly the recurring-revenue line that supports a premium multiple**: services and software revenue was **flat YoY at $246M**, and within the larger Enterprise Visibility & Mobility (EVM) segment it *declined* ~1.4%. Management simultaneously guided Q2 EPS down to $3.00–$3.50 and cut full-year profitability, attributing the reduction entirely to U.S. import tariffs. A model that pays for an EPS beat while the software/services growth engine stalls and incremental tariff cost hits gross margin justifies multiple compression independent of the beat.

## 1. Quality of the beat (facts)

Per the Q1 press release and earnings slides ([zebra.com, 2025-04-29](https://www.zebra.com/us/en/about-zebra/newsroom/press-releases/2025/zebra-technologies-announces-first-quarter-2025-results.html); [1Q25 slides, 2025-04-29](https://s23.q4cdn.com/838148541/files/doc_financials/2025/q1/1Q25-earnings-slides-Final.pdf)): gross margin 49.3% GAAP / 49.6% adjusted (vs. 47.9% / 48.1%); adjusted EBITDA $292M, 22.3% margin (+240bps); GAAP net income $136M / $2.62 diluted. Free cash flow was $158M ($178M operating cash flow less $20M capex), with $125M of buybacks and the $62M Photoneo acquisition.

But the composition matters: **tangible products grew 14.3% ($1,062M vs. $929M) while services and software grew 0.0% ($246M vs. $246M)**, so recurring revenue fell from 20.9% to 18.8% of sales ([10-Q, filed for period ended 2025-03-29](https://s23.q4cdn.com/838148541/files/doc_financials/2025/q1/1f35b441-31aa-47a7-90c0-411b0f5aedd1.pdf)). Segment detail: AIT $462M (+17.9%), EVM $846M (+8.0%); EVM services & software $216M vs. $219M (**−1.4%**). Remaining performance obligations slipped to **$1.18B from $1.19B** at year-end 2024. Zebra's own summary concedes "modest growth in Services and Software."

## 2. Debt-to-equity risk (facts + calculation)

At 2025-03-29: total assets $7,899M; total liabilities $4,273M; **total stockholders' equity $3,626M**; current portion of long-term debt $69M plus long-term debt $2,103M (= $2,172M), which Zebra describes as **total debt of $2,183M** against **$879M cash**.

Computed ratios (my arithmetic on the above):
- **Total debt / book equity ≈ 0.60×**; net debt / equity ≈ 0.36×; total liabilities / equity ≈ **1.18×**.
- Goodwill $3,927M + other intangibles $423M = $4,350M **exceeds** book equity → **tangible equity ≈ −$724M** (≈ −$14/share).
- Company-stated leverage: **1.2× net debt / adjusted EBITDA**; $1.5B revolver capacity; implied LTM adjusted EBITDA ≈ $1.09B.
- **Interest expense, net rose to $23M from $17M (+35%)** on essentially flat debt, and Q1 net-debt/EBITDA coverage of ~12.7× (EBITDA/net interest) is comfortable.

**Interpretation.** Reported 0.60× debt-to-equity is not distressed. The risk is *structural*: equity is an acquisition-intangible residual (2015 Motorola enterprise deal, matrox, Photoneo), so leverage on tangible capital is undefined/negative, and the denominator is being deliberately shrunk by buybacks — treasury stock rose from $(1,900)M to $(2,025)M in one quarter, and CFO Nathan Winters cited **$200M repurchased year-to-date through April** ([Investing.com transcript, 2025-06-04/05](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/zebra-at-45th-annual-william-blair-growth-stock-conference-strategic-growth-plans-93CH-4081977)). Continued repurchase against ~$700M+ FCF guidance mechanically raises debt-to-equity even with no new borrowing. If an acquirer or lender applies a haircut for goodwill impairment risk, equity support for the $2.18B stack is thin. Rising interest expense (+35%) at a time when the 10-year Treasury stood at **4.47%** and 2-year at **4.01%** on 2025-06-10 (Fed H.15 via OpenBB; retrospective observation, not a frozen vintage) compounds both refinancing cost and discount-rate pressure.

## 3. Why that justifies a valuation cut (mechanism)

1. **Mix determines multiple.** Hardware-led growth (+14.3% tangible, cyclically driven, tariff-exposed) with zero software/services growth is a lower-quality revenue mix than the "software-ification" narrative that supports a premium. Flat recurring revenue and slightly lower RPO argue against extrapolating the Q1 beat.
2. **Guidance implies peak earnings.** Q2 was guided to ~19% adjusted EBITDA margin (−330bps from Q1) and EPS $3.00–$3.50, i.e. ~**−19% sequentially** at midpoint. FY25 EPS guidance of $13.75–$14.75 sits ~**11% below** a naive Q1 annualisation ($16.08). A "beat" with a forward cut is a negative revision event for consensus.
3. **Tariff cost is a margin transfer, not a one-off.** Zebra quantified **$80–90M annualised gross-profit impact** (FY25 ≈ $70M, **incremental $50M versus prior guidance**) and expected only ~$80M of offsetting U.S. price adjustments. At a ~$13.5B market cap (50.85M shares as of 2025-04-22 × $265.37 on 2025-05-08, a price implied by Form 4 award valuation), ~$50–70M of lost gross profit is ~0.4% of market cap pre-tax but ~3–5% of EPS — enough to move a target by tens of dollars.
4. **Arithmetic of a price-target cut.** A $50 change in a target equals ≈ $2.5B of market value, about **2.4× annual adjusted EBITDA** — meaning large target reductions are almost always *multiple* decisions. This is why a Hold-rated firm (a $296-type target circulating in later aggregator pages) and a Buy-rated firm (a $346→$410-type target) can differ by ~15% on identical estimates.

## 4. Counterarguments to the bear case

- Liquidity is genuinely strong: $879M cash, $1.5B undrawn revolver, capital-light model (capex <1.5% of sales, ~80% of volume via channel partners, outsourced manufacturing), 1.2× net leverage — Zebra framed itself "well equipped to navigate" the trade environment.
- Diversification offsets: EMEA +18%, LatAm +18%, APAC +13% organic; RFID approaching $200M revenue with expected double-digit 2H growth; supply chain from China cut to ~30% of North America volume; a 10% NA price increase worth ~$50M in 2025 — all cited by the CFO on 2025-06-04.
- Insider filings through the cutoff show **no open-market selling** by officers/directors (Finnhub Form 4 extract: only code "A" awards, code "F" tax-withholding disposals, and ~4,414 shares bought by director Anders Gustafsson at ~$310 on 2025-02-24/25) — consistent with accumulation into the April–May drawdown.

## 5. Scenarios (forward, explicitly not fact)

- **Base:** FY25 lands mid-guide (sales ~$5.2B, EPS ~$14.25); tariff cost largely mitigated by pricing; 2H cycle recovery keeps software/services flat. Fair value clusters near consensus; multiple compresses modestly as growth quality, not growth rate, sets the price.
- **Bear:** tariffs exceed $90M annualised net, pricing partially fails, and services/software stays flat for 2–3 quarters → both estimates and multiple fall; goodwill ($3.93B vs. $3.63B equity) becomes the talking point and refinancing of the ~$2.1B notes at >4% costs ~$85–95M annual interest vs. $23M net booked in Q1.
- **Bull:** EVM recurring revenue re-accelerates with RPO rising above $1.2B and RFID/machine vision hit double digits → Q1's hardware-led mix is re-rated upward.
