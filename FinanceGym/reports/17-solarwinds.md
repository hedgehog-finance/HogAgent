# SolarWinds: Reconciling a ~$4.4 Billion Take-Private with January 2025 Analyst Targets

## 1. Direct answer

The take-private **did not align** with SolarWinds' analyst targets — it **exceeded all of them**. SolarWinds' own merger document lists a **median analyst price target of $15.00** alongside undisturbed market benchmarks, against a **$18.50 per-share cash merger consideration** (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1739942/000173994225000035/swidefm14cinformationstate.htm). That is **+23.3%** vs. the median target, **+28.4%** vs. the 1-month undisturbed VWAP of $14.41, **+31.6%** vs. the 3-month VWAP of $14.06, and **+21.9%** vs. the 52-week undisturbed high of $15.18.

The two "warning" signals in the question cut in opposite directions from the usual reading. Q4 2024 executive sales were executed at **$13.00–$13.68**, i.e. **26%–30% below** the eventual take-out — behaviour inconsistent with informed selling ahead of a premium bid, but highly consistent with weak standalone conviction. That conviction level is visible in the price itself: a Form 4 tax-withholding reference price of **$14.15 on 2025-01-04** means the stock traded ~**5.7% below** the $15.00 median target in early January 2025 (https://finnhub.io/api/v1/stock/insider-transactions?symbol=SWI&from=2024-10-01&to=2025-04-23). Conclusion: the gap between the deal and the target set is best explained as **control value plus sponsor willingness to pay a high EBITDA multiple**, not as information that sell-side targets had already captured.

## 4. Insider selling: what the filings actually show

Everything labelled selling at other dates is **non-discretionary**: code "F" share-withholding for taxes (Nov 15, 2024 @ $13.18; Jan 4, 2025 @ $14.15; Feb 15/18, 2025 @ $18.30/$18.32) and **code "G" gifts** on Apr 8, 2025 (−264,278 and −172,522, price $0). The Feb 15–18 withholding prices show the stock re-rated to within ~1% of the deal price immediately after announcement.

**Mechanism:** if executives had anticipated a February control bid, selling in October–November at $13 was economically irrational. The signal is therefore **negative for standalone expectations, not negative for deal fairness**. Conversely, there is no admitted evidence of distribution *into* the deal price, which weakens the "heavy insider selling = deal is cheap" thesis.

## 8. Bottom line

Alignment was **low by design**: the take-private cleared every January/February 2025 analyst and market benchmark. Executive selling at $13 and any institutional rotation describe **the standalone story the market was being asked to give up**, not a case that $4.4B was too low — the informational content of those signals is a *bearish* standalone view, priced at a discount to targets, resolved by a sponsor paying above every pre-deal reference including the 52-week high.
