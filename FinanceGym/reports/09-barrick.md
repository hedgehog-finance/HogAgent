# Mali's Fiscal Demands, Executive Detentions and Barrick (GOLD): Discount Arithmetic and Loulo-Gounkoto Viability

*Point-in-time research as at 2025-04-28. All facts below are from sources published on or before that date. Calculations labelled "author's estimate" derive from those admitted inputs plus explicitly stated assumptions.*

## Direct answer

As of the cutoff, the $160 million Resolute Mining settlement and Barrick's negotiated $438 million payment are best read as **the price of re-entry into Mali's new fiscal regime rather than a one-time legal cost**. They will not, by themselves, move Barrick's valuation much: $438m is roughly 1% of Barrick's late-April 2025 equity value and about 0.9 years of Loulo-Gounkoto's attributable 2024 EBITDA (author's estimate). The valuation discount is driven by something else — the market's inability to underwrite Malian cash flows at face value because the rules, not the bill, changed (state stake up to 30–35%, royalties 6%→10.5%). Loulo-Gounkoto remains **physically and geologically viable** (578koz produced in 2024, 5.13 g/t Q4 grade, $828/oz cash costs), so it is economic at any plausible gold price; the open question is **what share of that margin Barrick keeps**. Critically, as of April 28 the deal had *not* been executed by Bamako: Barrick said on April 15 that Mali closed its national office, threatened expropriation and blocked the agreement it had accepted in February, so the discount was still widening, not closing.

## What the settlements cost, in evidence

**Resolute (November 2024).** Resolute agreed to pay $160m — $80m immediately, the balance in coming months — after CEO Terry Holohan and two staff were detained on November 8–9 while travelling to Bamako for negotiations; the executives were released (~November 20) under a memorandum of understanding requiring "all outstanding claims against the company in Mali to be settled" ([ABC News, 18 Nov 2024](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-18/resolute-mining-to-pay-mali-government-after-executives-detained/104614270); [CEOWORLD, 21 Nov 2024](https://ceoworld.biz/2024-11-21/resolute-mining-executives-released-in-mali-after-160-million-settlement); [Reuters, 19 Feb 2025](https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/barrick-gold-signs-agreement-with-mali-end-mining-dispute-2025-02-19)). Mali's position was that Resolute owed $161m identified in a sector-wide audit ([CEOWORLD](https://ceoworld.biz/2024-11-21/resolute-mining-executives-released-in-mali-after-160-million-settlement)). Resolute's Q4 report (30 Jan 2025) shows the cash hit (drawn overdraft of $34.2m) and — more importantly — quantifies the *recurring* burden: Syama's 2025 AISC guidance of $1,700–1,800/oz is inflated by "an additional ~$250/oz or around $175/oz at a Group level" purely from transitioning to the 2023 Mining Code ([Resolute activities report](https://www.rml.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025-01-250130-RSG-ASX-December-Quarterly-Report-FINAL.pdf)). At the guided ~202.5koz midpoint, $250/oz ≈ **$50.6m/yr of permanent cost** (author's estimate) — i.e. the annualised regime cost alone approaches a third of the headline $160m settlement every year.

**Barrick (February–April 2025).** Under the agreement Barrick signed, it would pay 275 billion CFA (~$438m) in return for release of detained employees, return of seized gold and restart of Loulo-Gounkoto ([Reuters, 19 Feb 2025](https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/barrick-gold-signs-agreement-with-mali-end-mining-dispute-2025-02-19)). Timeline of coercion: four senior Loulo-Gounkoto employees briefly detained 27 Sept 2024, released 1 Oct, re-arrested and charged 25 Nov 2024 and held awaiting trial; an arrest warrant was reported for CEO Mark Bristow and the complex's GM on 5 Dec 2024; gold shipments blocked 16 Dec; ~3 tonnes (~$245m) seized 11 Jan 2025 and moved to the state-owned Banque Malienne de Solidarité, which Mali planned to liquidate to fund operations; operations suspended 14 Jan 2025 ([CIM Magazine, 17 Apr 2025](https://magazine.cim.org/en/news/2025/mali-closes-barrick-gold-office-amid-ongoing-mining-dispute-en); [Reuters, 26 Nov 2024](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/four-barrick-gold-employees-detained-mali-sources-say-2024-11-26); [TRT Afrika, 15 Jan 2025](https://www.trtafrika.com/english/article/1c6a3e09e3d7)). Barrick had already paid $85m in October 2024 under a framework, and $460m to the Malian state during 2024 ([CIM](https://magazine.cim.org/en/news/2025/mali-closes-barrick-gold-office-amid-ongoing-mining-dispute-en); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/barrick-gold-signs-agreement-with-mali-end-mining-dispute-2025-02-19)). *Whether the $85m sits inside the $438m is not stated in the admitted sources — an unresolved evidence gap.*

## Mechanism: why the discount persists

1. **Rule change, not bill change.** The August 2023 code raised the state's stake (10% with an option to buy a further 20%), and reporting in February 2025 describes a new law requiring divestment of 35% of new projects to Malian investors and royalties rising from ~6% to 10.5% ([CIM](https://magazine.cim.org/en/news/2025/mali-closes-barrick-gold-office-amid-ongoing-mining-dispute-en); [EY, April 2025](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-au/insights/mining-metals/documents/ey-the-2025-risks-and-opportunities-for-the-gold-mining-sector-240425-002.pdf)). Applying the 6%→10.5% delta to Barrick's Q4 realised price of $2,657/oz is ~$120/oz, or ~$55m/yr attributable at 2024 volumes; a move from 80% to 70% economic ownership would cede ~58koz attributable (author's estimates from [Barrick Q4 2024 presentation](https://s25.q4cdn.com/322814910/files/doc_presentations/2025/02/Barrick_Q4_2024_Results_Presentation.pdf)). Capitalising a plausible combined erosion of ~$150m/yr for 8 years at 8% gives ≈ **$862m** — roughly twice the headline settlement. The settlement is the entry fee; the fiscal architecture is the annuity.
2. **Enforcement risk is not diversifiable.** Detention of executives as a negotiating instrument and seizure of inventory convert a contractual dispute into a *property-rights* discount. Barrick itself booked a **$484m goodwill impairment** on the Loulo-Gounkoto CGU (carrying value $3,564m vs fair value less costs of disposal $3,080m — a 13.6% write-down) and recorded the ICSID filing of 18 Dec 2024 ([Q4 MD&A](https://minedocs.com/28/Barrick-MDA-2024.pdf); [Q4 presentation](https://s25.q4cdn.com/322814910/files/doc_presentations/2025/02/Barrick_Q4_2024_Results_Presentation.pdf)).
3. **The market already priced Mali at or below zero.** Barrick's February 2025 deck states that "market based multiples imply negative value for Barrick's assets outside of NGM and copper," naming Loulo-Gounkoto and Kibali ([Q4 presentation](https://s25.q4cdn.com/322814910/files/doc_presentations/2025/02/Barrick_Q4_2024_Results_Presentation.pdf)). Against FY2024 attributable EBITDA of $5,185m and $45.52bn market capitalisation on 24 April ([Globe and Mail, 24 Apr 2025](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/stocks/ABX-T/pressreleases/32031112/barrick-gold-stock-analysis-q1-2025-earnings-and-future-growth-prospects)), EV/EBITDA was ≈8.9x (author's estimate) — while, per January reporting, the stock was down ~12% over the prior year as gold rose ~30% ([Motley Fool Canada, 15 Jan 2025](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/barrick-gold-stock-buy-sell-164500930.html)). Consistently, the $438m news produced only a **+3.37% TSX move** ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/barrick-gold-signs-agreement-with-mali-end-mining-dispute-2025-02-19)): resolution was already assumed.

## Mechanism: Loulo-Gounkoto's operational viability

The orebody case is strong: 578koz produced in 2024 (100% basis) at 4.73 g/t average grade, 91% recovery, $828/oz total cash costs, $1,218/oz cost of sales, EBITDA $598m, capex $307m. Q4 income was already negative (–$13m) solely because export restrictions cut sales to 47koz against 156koz produced ([Q4 presentation](https://s25.q4cdn.com/322814910/files/doc_presentations/2025/02/Barrick_Q4_2024_Results_Presentation.pdf)). Attributable output is ~11.8% of Barrick's 2024 gold production and Mali was ~8.3% of revenue ($1.07bn / $12.92bn) — material, not existential (author's estimates). The viability threat is operational decay, not geology: guidance was cut to 3.2–3.5Moz from 3.9Moz, Loulo-Gounkoto was excluded from 2025 guidance entirely, and the five-year plan assumed restart only "from March 1, 2025." Sustained stoppages degrade reserve sequencing, workforce and contractor capacity, while Mali's own industrial gold output fell 23% y/y in 2024 — evidence the value destruction is bilateral.

## Counterarguments

- **Mali needs the revenue.** Barrick contributed $460m in 2024 and was expected to add ~$550m in 2025; a shuttered mine is a self-harming negotiating tactic ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/commodities/barrick-gold-signs-agreement-with-mali-end-mining-dispute-2025-02-19)).
- **Cooperation restores access.** B2Gold settled a tax dispute in September 2024 and kept operating ([CIM](https://magazine.cim.org/en/news/2025/mali-closes-barrick-gold-office-amid-ongoing-mining-dispute-en)).
- **The discount is multi-causal.** Nevada Gold Mines' accounting structure, uncredited copper optionality and sector-wide equity-gold decoupling may explain more of the de-rating than Mali alone.
- **Legal leverage is real.** An ICSID claim plus Mali's need for foreign capital raises the cost of expropriation ([African Law & Business, 13 Jan 2025](https://www.africanlawbusiness.com/news/mali-faces-gold-mining-arbitration)).
